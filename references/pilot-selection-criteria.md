# Pilot Selection Criteria

Use these criteria to assess whether a proposed workflow is suitable
for a limited pilot. Each criterion should be evaluated as Met,
Partially met, Not met, or N/A.

## Workflow Evidence

- [ ] The proposal is based on evidence of how the work currently
  happens, not only on a desired outcome
- [ ] The current process has been observed or described by someone
  who performs it
- [ ] Time, effort, or quality baselines exist for the current process
- [ ] The proposal identifies what is repeated, time-consuming, or
  frustrating about the current workflow

## Staff Familiarity with Current Process

- [ ] The people who currently do the work have been consulted
- [ ] Experienced staff can describe the steps, exceptions, and
  quality standards of the current process
- [ ] The proposal reflects practical constraints known to staff,
  not only idealized workflow assumptions

## Workflow Decomposition

- [ ] The workflow has been broken into individual steps
- [ ] Each step has been assigned to the appropriate type of support:
  generative AI, search/retrieval, templates, deterministic
  automation, or human judgment
- [ ] Steps that require human judgment are clearly identified
- [ ] Steps that could use simpler tools (templates, automation,
  search) are not assigned to generative AI without justification

## Small and Reversible Scope

- [ ] The pilot involves a limited number of participants (within
  organizational pilot limits)
- [ ] The pilot has a defined end date
- [ ] The pilot can be stopped without lasting harm to operations
- [ ] The pilot does not become a de facto production deployment

## Fast Testing and Feedback

- [ ] The pilot can produce initial results within a short timeframe
  (days to weeks, not months)
- [ ] A method for collecting feedback from participants is defined
- [ ] Feedback will be reviewed and acted upon during the pilot,
  not only after it ends
- [ ] The pilot timeline accounts for a learning period

## Involvement of Experienced Staff

- [ ] At least one person experienced in the current workflow is
  involved in the pilot design
- [ ] Experienced staff will review AI output during the pilot
- [ ] Experienced staff can flag quality issues, exceptions, and
  edge cases
- [ ] The proposal does not bypass experienced staff in favor of
  faster rollout

## Learning Value for Future Workflows

- [ ] The pilot is designed to produce transferable lessons
- [ ] Success and failure cases will be documented
- [ ] The workflow type (drafting, retrieval, etc.) is one that
  could apply to other teams or processes
- [ ] Knowledge-sharing value is noted but is not the sole reason
  for the pilot

## Ability to Document and Share Lessons

- [ ] There is a plan for recording what was learned during the pilot
- [ ] Lessons will be stored in a format others can review
- [ ] Lessons will be distinguished from informal observations
- [ ] A method for periodic review of accumulated knowledge exists

## Reviewer Workload

- [ ] The workload for human reviewers has been estimated
- [ ] Reviewer workload is realistic given existing responsibilities
- [ ] The pilot does not create more review work than the current
  process requires
- [ ] The efficiency gain is not achieved by shifting effort to
  reviewers rather than reducing total effort

## Employee Recognition and Incentives

- [ ] Employees contributing to the pilot have a reason to participate
  beyond obligation
- [ ] Contributions to workflow improvement will be recognized
- [ ] The pilot does not punish employees for identifying problems
  with the AI output
- [ ] Participation is voluntary where possible
