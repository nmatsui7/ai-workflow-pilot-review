# Knowledge Review Guidance

Workflow observations accumulate during pilots and day-to-day operations.
Not all observations are equally reliable or reusable. This file
describes how observations may progress through maturity stages
before they are treated as validated, reusable knowledge.

## Knowledge Maturity Stages

### Stage 1: Informal Observation

**Description:** An individual notes something about a workflow
based on personal experience, a single incident, or an unverified
impression.

**Required evidence:** None beyond the observation itself.

**Required reviewer:** None (self-recorded).

**Permitted use:** Internal reference only. May be used to generate
hypotheses or identify areas for further investigation.

**Limitations:** May be incomplete, biased, context-dependent, or
incorrect. Must not be treated as validated knowledge.

**Review or stale-date requirements:** None required, but should be
reviewed before reuse, or according to a stale-review period defined
and approved by the domain owner.

**Conditions for promotion:** Must be supported by at least one
additional evidence source or observation to move to Stage 2.

---

### Stage 2: Evidence-Supported Finding

**Description:** An observation is supported by at least one
additional evidence source, such as a time measurement, a comparison,
a second observer, or documented data.

**Required evidence:** At least one source beyond the original
observation. Evidence should be identifiable and reviewable.

**Required reviewer:** The person who recorded the finding should
verify that the evidence supports the claim.

**Permitted use:** May be referenced in pilot reviews, workflow
assessments, and internal discussions. Must be labeled as
"evidence-supported" with source references.

**Limitations:** Evidence may be limited in scope, time-bound, or
context-specific. Must not be generalized beyond the context in
which it was gathered.

**Review or stale-date requirements:** Should be reviewed before
reuse, or according to a stale-review period defined and approved
by the domain owner. Evidence should be rechecked if the workflow
or context changes.

**Conditions for promotion:** Must be reviewed by someone other
than the original author and found to be accurate and appropriately
contextualized to move to Stage 3.

---

### Stage 3: Reviewed Workflow Lesson

**Description:** An evidence-supported finding has been reviewed
by at least one person other than the original author and confirmed
to be accurate, appropriately contextualized, and clearly stated.

**Required evidence:** Evidence from Stage 2, plus reviewer
confirmation.

**Required reviewer:** At least one reviewer who is familiar with
the workflow or a similar workflow.

**Permitted use:** May be used to inform pilot design, workflow
reviews, and training materials. Must include reviewer attribution
and review date.

**Limitations:** Reviewed lessons are valid in the context in which
they were reviewed. Changes in tools, processes, or team composition
may affect applicability.

**Review or stale-date requirements:** Should be reviewed according
to a schedule defined and approved by the domain owner, or when the
underlying workflow changes significantly.

**Conditions for promotion:** Must be validated across multiple
contexts or confirmed by multiple reviewers to move to Stage 4.

---

### Stage 4: Approved Reusable Example

**Description:** A reviewed lesson that has been validated across
multiple contexts or confirmed by multiple reviewers and is suitable
for reuse in similar workflows.

**Required evidence:** Evidence from Stage 3, plus confirmation
from multiple contexts or reviewers.

**Required reviewer:** At least two reviewers familiar with the
workflow type, or one reviewer with cross-context validation.

**Permitted use:** May be referenced in pilot proposals, workflow
templates, and training materials as a validated example. Must
include attribution, review date, and context of validity.

**Limitations:** Approved examples are not organizational policy.
They are reference guidance that should be adapted to local context.
They do not replace legal, compliance, or HR review.

**Review or stale-date requirements:** Should be reviewed according
to a schedule defined and approved by the domain owner, or when the
underlying technology, process, or regulatory environment changes.

**Conditions for promotion:** May be incorporated into formal
playbooks, SKILL.md instructions, or retrieval sources if it
meets the standards for those formats.

---

### Stage 5: Playbook, SKILL.md Instruction, or Retrieval Source

**Description:** An approved reusable example that has been
incorporated into a formal, maintainable knowledge artifact such
as a playbook, a SKILL.md instruction, or a retrieval source for
RAG-style systems.

**Required evidence:** Evidence from Stage 4, plus conformance
with the format and quality standards of the target artifact.

**Required reviewer:** The artifact owner or a designated reviewer
with authority over the target format.

**Permitted use:** May be used as authoritative guidance within
the scope defined by the artifact. Must be maintained and reviewed
on the schedule defined by the artifact.

**Limitations:** Even formal artifacts require periodic review.
They are valid only as long as the underlying assumptions hold.
They do not replace human judgment for novel or ambiguous situations.

**Review or stale-date requirements:** Per the artifact's review
schedule, as defined and approved by the domain owner.

**Conditions for promotion:** This is the terminal stage.
No further promotion is needed.

---

## Key Principles

1. **Accumulated AI-generated notes are not automatically validated
   knowledge.** They must progress through the stages before being
   treated as reusable.

2. **Informal observations must be clearly separated from reviewed
   findings.** Label all observations with their current stage.

3. **Review is a human responsibility.** AI can help organize and
   present observations, but review and validation require human
   judgment.

4. **Context matters.** A finding valid in one team, tool, or process
   may not apply elsewhere. Always note the context of validity.

5. **Stale knowledge is worse than no knowledge.** Expired findings
   should be flagged and rechecked before reuse.

## Domain-Owner Adaptation

These five stages are independent of any specific documentation
hierarchy or knowledge-management system. Domain owners may map them
to their own approval systems, document types, and review processes.
The stages describe maturity conditions, not specific tools or
formats.
