# Adoption Risk Types

Each risk type includes a definition, observable signals, possible
impact, discovery questions, mitigation examples, and escalation
indicators. Mitigation examples are possible practices, not mandatory
organization policy.

## Assessment Timing

Adoption risks should be assessed at four points:

1. **During initial review** — identify risks before the pilot is
   approved.
2. **Immediately before pilot launch** — confirm mitigations are in
   place and conditions have not changed.
3. **During the pilot if material conditions change** — reassess if
   scope, participants, tools, data, or constraints change in a way
   that affects identified risks.
4. **At pilot completion** — evaluate which risks materialized, what
   mitigations worked, and what lessons should be recorded.

Do not impose quarterly or semi-annual reviews as a universal
requirement. Review frequency should match the pilot's risk profile
and duration.

## Workload Increase

**Definition:** The AI workflow adds tasks, review burden, or
maintenance work rather than reducing total effort.

**Observable signals:**
- Efficiency gains are claimed but reviewer workload is not estimated
- The proposal increases output volume without reducing input effort
- Staff must learn new tools, formats, or processes on top of existing
  duties

**Possible impact:**
- Staff resentment; burnout; reduced quality; pilot abandonment

**Discovery questions:**
- How many items will reviewers need to process per day or week?
- Is the total time spent (AI + human) less than the current process?
- Who absorbs the work if the AI output is poor?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Estimate reviewer workload before launch
- Cap pilot volume to manageable levels
- Build learning period into the timeline

**Escalation indicators:**
- Reviewer workload exceeds current process effort
- No estimate of review burden exists

## Unclear Expectations

**Definition:** Participants do not know what they are expected to do,
how to use the AI output, or what quality standard to apply.

**Observable signals:**
- No role descriptions for pilot participants
- Quality criteria for AI output are undefined
- Success metrics are vague ("make things better")

**Possible impact:**
- Inconsistent usage; unreliable evaluation data; frustration

**Discovery questions:**
- What exactly are participants expected to do with AI output?
- What does "acceptable quality" mean in concrete terms?
- How will participants know if they are using the workflow correctly?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Define specific roles and expectations before launch
- Provide quality criteria with examples
- Create a short usage guide

**Escalation indicators:**
- No quality criteria exist
- Participants cannot describe their role in the pilot

## Staff Trust

**Definition:** Employees do not trust the AI tool, the process, or
the intent behind the pilot.

**Observable signals:**
- Staff express skepticism about AI capabilities or motives
- Participants avoid using the tool or use it perfunctorily
- Negative past experiences with technology changes

**Possible impact:**
- Low adoption; dishonest feedback; pilot produces unreliable data

**Discovery questions:**
- Have staff expressed concerns about this or similar initiatives?
- Is there a history of technology changes that increased workload
  without benefit?
- Do staff understand why this pilot is being conducted?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Communicate the purpose and limits of the pilot clearly
- Make participation voluntary where possible
- Share early results transparently, including problems

**Escalation indicators:**
- Staff refuse to participate
- Trust issues are widespread and unaddressed

## Role Confusion

**Definition:** Multiple people are involved but no one is clearly
responsible for oversight, exceptions, escalation, or quality control.

**Observable signals:**
- "Everyone is responsible" without specific assignments
- No one is designated to handle AI errors or edge cases
- Oversight is assumed rather than documented

**Possible impact:**
- Errors go undetected; escalations are ignored; the pilot fails
  without a clear cause

**Discovery questions:**
- Who reviews AI output before it is used?
- Who handles it when the AI output is wrong?
- Who monitors the pilot and reports results?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Assign specific roles: reviewer, exception handler, pilot owner
- Document who is responsible for each workflow step
- Define escalation paths

**Escalation indicators:**
- No specific roles are assigned
- Exception handling is not addressed

## Training Gap

**Definition:** Participants are not prepared to use the AI workflow
effectively due to insufficient training, unclear instructions, or
assumed knowledge.

**Observable signals:**
- Training is described as "intuitive" or "self-explanatory"
- No training materials or timeline are planned
- The workflow assumes technical knowledge participants may not have

**Possible impact:**
- Low adoption; incorrect usage; frustration; negative perception
  of AI tools

**Discovery questions:**
- What training is planned before the pilot starts?
- How long will it take participants to become comfortable?
- Are training materials appropriate for the audience?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Provide a short training session or guide
- Build a learning period into the pilot timeline
- Identify a go-to person for questions

**Escalation indicators:**
- No training plan exists
- Workflow assumes knowledge that participants may not have

## Lack of Recognition

**Definition:** Employees contribute to the pilot or workflow
improvement without acknowledgment, credit, or incentive.

**Observable signals:**
- Pilot is presented as a requirement or opportunity without
  considering the employee's perspective
- No mention of how contributions will be recognized
- Employee time and effort are treated as free resources

**Possible impact:**
- Low engagement; resentment; selective participation; unreliable
  evaluation data

**Discovery questions:**
- How will employee contributions be recognized?
- Is participation voluntary or assigned?
- Will employees be credited for improvements they suggest?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Acknowledge contributions publicly
- Consider workload adjustments for pilot participants
- Provide feedback on how employee input shaped the pilot

**Escalation indicators:**
- No recognition or incentive is described
- Participation is mandated without accommodation

## Evaluation Ambiguity

**Definition:** The pilot lacks clear success criteria, measurement
methods, or a plan for acting on results.

**Observable signals:**
- Success metrics are vague or unmeasurable
- No baseline exists for comparison
- No plan for what happens after the pilot ends

**Possible impact:**
- Inconclusive results; wasted effort; inability to make informed
  decisions about scaling

**Discovery questions:**
- What specific metric will determine success?
- What is the current baseline for that metric?
- What happens if the pilot meets its goal? Fails to meet it?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Define measurable success criteria with baselines
- Plan data collection before launch
- Define decision criteria for post-pilot actions

**Escalation indicators:**
- No measurable success criteria exist
- No baseline is available for comparison

---

## Weak Knowledge Sharing

**Definition:** Lessons learned during the pilot are not documented,
shared, or made available for future workflows.

**Observable signals:**
- No plan for recording what was learned
- Lessons exist only in individual memory
- Informal observations are treated as validated knowledge

**Possible impact:**
- Repeated mistakes; missed opportunities for reuse; organizational
  learning does not accumulate

**Discovery questions:**
- How will lessons be recorded during the pilot?
- Where will lessons be stored?
- How will others learn from this pilot?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Define a simple method for recording lessons
- Store lessons in a shared, reviewable format
- Distinguish informal observations from reviewed findings

**Escalation indicators:**
- No plan for knowledge sharing exists
- Informal notes are treated as formal policy

## Insufficient Involvement of Experienced Staff

**Definition:** The pilot is designed or evaluated without input from
people who currently perform the work.

**Observable signals:**
- Experienced staff were not consulted during proposal development
- The proposal reflects idealized assumptions about the workflow
- Quality standards and exceptions are not identified by practitioners

**Possible impact:**
- The workflow misses critical steps; quality drops; exceptions are
  not handled; experienced staff disengage

**Discovery questions:**
- Were experienced staff consulted during proposal development?
- Will experienced staff review AI output during the pilot?
- Can experienced staff describe the exceptions and edge cases?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Include at least one experienced practitioner in pilot design
- Require experienced staff to review a sample of AI output
- Document exceptions and edge cases identified by staff

**Escalation indicators:**
- No experienced staff were consulted
- Quality standards are defined without practitioner input

## Resistance from Efficiency Gains Becoming Additional Workload

**Definition:** Efficiency gains from AI are absorbed as additional
output expectations rather than reducing staff burden.

**Observable signals:**
- The proposal claims efficiency gains but does not reduce existing
  workload
- AI output creates new tasks (review, correction, follow-up) that
  did not exist before
- The expectation is "do more" rather than "do the same with less
  effort"

**Possible impact:**
- Staff perceive AI as a tool for increasing workload, not reducing
  it; resistance; reduced morale

**Discovery questions:**
- Will efficiency gains reduce existing workload or increase output
  expectations?
- Who benefits from the efficiency gain — staff, management, or
  customers?
- Has staff been asked what they would do with recovered time?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Clarify that efficiency gains should reduce burden, not just
  increase output
- Ask staff what they would do with recovered time
- Measure workload impact alongside output metrics

**Escalation indicators:**
- Efficiency gains are presented as output increases
- Staff have no voice in how recovered time is used

---

## Unclear Ownership of Workflow Improvements

**Definition:** No one is responsible for maintaining, updating, or
governing the AI workflow after the pilot ends.

**Observable signals:**
- The pilot has an end date but no post-pilot ownership plan
- No one is assigned to monitor ongoing performance
- Maintenance and updates are not addressed

**Possible impact:**
- The workflow degrades over time; problems accumulate; the pilot
  does not lead to sustainable improvement

**Discovery questions:**
- Who will own the workflow after the pilot ends?
- How will ongoing performance be monitored?
- What happens when the AI model or tools are updated?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Assign post-pilot ownership before the pilot starts
- Define monitoring and maintenance responsibilities
- Plan for model or tool updates

**Escalation indicators:**
- No post-pilot ownership plan exists
- Maintenance is not addressed

---

## Premature Reuse of Unreviewed Knowledge

**Definition:** Informal observations or AI-generated notes from the
pilot are treated as validated, reusable knowledge without proper
review.

**Observable signals:**
- AI-generated notes are shared as "findings" without review
- Informal observations are added to knowledge bases without
  verification
- Pilot lessons are presented as established best practices

**Possible impact:**
- Incorrect or context-dependent advice is reused inappropriately;
  quality degrades; trust in knowledge sharing is reduced

**Discovery questions:**
- Have pilot lessons been reviewed by someone other than the
  original author?
- Are informal observations clearly labeled as such?
- Is there a process for promoting observations to reviewed lessons?

**Mitigation examples:**
- Label informal observations clearly
- Require review before lessons are shared as guidance
- Use the knowledge maturity stages in
  `references/knowledge-review-guidance.md`

**Escalation indicators:**
- Unreviewed notes are shared as validated knowledge
- No review process exists for pilot lessons
